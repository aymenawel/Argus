# Filming and producing a polished, budget demo for Argus (scientific timelapse + app screen recording)

Scope: how to make side- and top-view desk-experiment footage (glasses, jars, petri dishes, plants) shot on a Nuroum V11 webcam or Canon XA50 camcorder look professional, and how to record and edit the app screen capture for a 3-minute judged demo (due Nov 4, 2026). Throughout, "measurement validity" notes flag anything that could change what Argus measures. Rule of thumb for the whole document: **anything done for looks (grading, deflicker, stabilisation, speed ramps, crops) is done only on a presentation copy; Argus analyses the original frames.**

Problems already seen in real runs, and where they are addressed below: plain grey wall background (Q2), arm passing in front (Q4, Q7), auto exposure settling (Q3), glare on glass (Q1), lamp changes (Q1, Q3).

## Q1. Lighting for glass, liquids and plants

### Takeaway
Light transparent vessels from behind or below through a diffuser (bright field: white glowing background, dark glass edges) or put them on black with light coming from behind and to the sides (dark field: bright rims on black); control reflections with black cards ("flags") and a polariser, and use only one kind of constant, flicker-free light in a room with daylight blocked, so nothing changes during a run. A cheap A4 LED tracing pad under a petri dish, or behind a jar through a diffuser, is the highest-value purchase.

### Cited Findings
**Bright field vs dark field for glass**
- Bright field: strong light on a white backdrop, or light shining *through* a translucent backdrop behind the glass; the glass edges are then defined by dark cards placed just outside the frame that act as "negative light" — [Sekonic: whiteline and blackline lighting](https://sekonic.com/classroom/whiteline-and-blackline-lighting/); [PopPhoto: using backlight to photograph glassware](https://www.popphoto.com/how-to/2014/07/how-to-using-backlight-to-photograph-glassware/); [Instructables: Photographing Glassware](https://instructables.com/Photographing-Glassware)
- Dark field: a black background shows through the body of the glass, and the edges are lit by light that goes behind a black card and bounces off reflectors (V-cards) — [DIY Photography: dark field](https://www.diyphotography.net/?p=819); [Matt Hughes: dark field photography](https://www.matthughesphoto.com/d-photography/dark-field-photography.html)
- A dark-field variant: leave the background unlit and put two narrow strip lights slightly behind the glass, one on each side, to draw clean bright rims — [PetaPixel stripbox articles](https://petapixel.com/tag/stripbox/); [LensesPro: how to photograph glass](https://lensespro.org/?p=31425)
- Dark-field lighting began in microscopy as a way to see nearly transparent objects — [Matt Hughes: dark field photography](https://www.matthughesphoto.com/d-photography/dark-field-photography.html)
- Matte black cards (flags) placed around the subject leave only a small gap for the camera to see into the "light envelope," which keeps unwanted reflections off the glass; move a small black card around and watch the glass for mirror-like hotspots — [Sekonic](https://sekonic.com/classroom/whiteline-and-blackline-lighting/); [CGS guide: how to photograph glass (PDF)](https://www.cgs.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/glass-how-to-photograph-cgs.pdf)
- Guides also recommend a tripod, a fairly small aperture and *very* clean glassware; dark-coloured glass needs stronger edge light and deeper flags — [Instructables](https://instructables.com/Photographing-Glassware); [LensesPro](https://lensespro.org/?p=31425)

**Backlighting liquids and translucent subjects with a light pad**
- An LED light pad is a thin panel giving even light across its surface, which makes it a steady backlight for small objects — [Photofocus: using a light pad for photography](https://photofocus.com/photography/lighting-photography/using-a-light-pad-for-photography/)
- Light pads work best with subjects that are already a little translucent (leaves, petals, thin fruit slices); hold the subject up to a light first to check — [Alessandra Chaves: botanical photography with a light pad](https://alessandrachaves.com/2024/07/12/botanical-photography-with-a-light-pad/); [Lori: lightpad photography in 5 steps](https://lori-tkhv.format.com/blog/lightpad-photography-5-easy-steps)
- One light-pad photographer overexposes by 2–3 stops for a clean bright background — [Alessandra Chaves](https://alessandrachaves.com/2024/07/12/botanical-photography-with-a-light-pad/)
- A flat pad suits flat or thin subjects; objects that are not flat (a jar, a plant) also need a secondary light from the front or side — [Photofocus](https://photofocus.com/photography/lighting-photography/using-a-light-pad-for-photography/)
- Backlit liquids should be fairly light in colour; light passes through white wine far more easily than red wine. For bottles and containers, a softbox or diffusion panel behind the subject is commonly recommended — [PhotographyTalk: back lighting foods and liquids](https://www.photographytalk.com/photography-articles/270-photography-tip-back-lighting-foods-and-liquids); [Ilex: backlighting in the studio](https://www.ilexinstant.com/backlighting-studio/)
- Light pads for tracing are sold as cheap consumer items, e.g. an A4 LED light pad listed for drawing/tracing/negatives — [Bunnings: Ozemu A4 LED light pad](https://www.bunnings.com.au/ozemu-a4-15in-led-light-pad-board-for-drawing-tracing-negative-photos_p0909047); [Adorama: light boxes for tracing](https://www.adorama.com/lists/light-boxes-for-tracing)

**Polarisers and glare**
- A circular polariser cuts reflections from non-metallic surfaces such as glass and water; you rotate it in front of the lens to dial the effect — [NYIP: how to use a polarizing filter](https://www.nyip.edu/photo-articles/archive/how-to-use-a-polarizing-filter); [Adorama B+W CPL listing](https://www.adorama.com/bw43cplm.html)
- The effect is strongest at roughly 30–40° to the reflecting surface (manufacturer claim) — [Kenko circular polarising filter](https://www.camera-warehouse.com.au/kenko-circular-polarising-filter)

**Plants: how a professional handles constant light**
- Neil Bromhall (former BBC scientific photographer) films plant timelapses in a blackened, windowless studio: a grow light acts as the sun, and a studio flash makes every exposure identical whatever the time of day — [PetaPixel: sunflower opening over 10 days](https://petapixel.com/2021/09/04/a-timelapse-of-a-sunflower-opening-over-10-days/)
- He engineered a blind that covers the grow lamp for every single exposure, so the grow light never appears in a frame — [DIY Photography: sunflower timelapse](https://www.diyphotography.net/?p=175094)
- High-end plant timelapses often combine techniques: the background can be shot separately over a short period and composited with the long growth sequence, which avoids lighting changes — [Unique Photo Q&A: plant time-lapses without wind and lighting changes](https://www.uniquephoto.com/community/qa/how-are-high-end-plant-growth-time-lapses-made-without-wind-and-lighting-changes)

**Flicker-free light**
- LED flicker/banding happens when the camera's frame rate or rolling shutter falls out of step with the LED's pulsing (PWM dimming or mains ripple) — [Enttec: why do LEDs flicker on camera](https://support.enttec.com/pixel/pixel-general-knowledge/why-do-leds-flicker-on-camera); [Ulanzi: ambient flicker guide](https://www.ulanzi.com/en-eu/blogs/knowledges/ambient-flicker-high-speed-outdoor-video-guide)
- Flicker-free (constant-current) LED drivers are the most reliable lighting fix; sources disagree on what PWM frequency is "enough" (forum answers range from 1,000–3,000 Hz to "20× the frame rate"), and a vendor warns no single number guarantees clean results because modulation depth and duty cycle matter too — [ControlBooth forum](https://www.controlbooth.com/threads/minimum-pwm-frequency-to-avoid-led-video-flicker.42667/latest); [Ulanzi: why LED panels flicker](https://www.ulanzi.com/blogs/knowledges/why-led-panels-flicker-120fps-240fps)
- Test the exact brightness/shutter/frame-rate combination you will shoot, changing one variable at a time; passing at one setting does not guarantee another — [Ulanzi: why LED panels flicker](https://www.ulanzi.com/blogs/knowledges/why-led-panels-flicker-120fps-240fps)

### Inferences
- **Recommended Argus lighting recipes (inferred from the above):**
  - *Petri dish, top view*: dish directly on an A4 LED tracing pad (bright field). Gives a uniform glowing background, high contrast for colonies, crystals, dye fronts and segmentation outlines, and no reflections because the light comes from below. Put a sheet of matte black card with a dish-sized hole cut in it over the pad if the bright surround overwhelms the frame.
  - *Glasses/jars, side view, coloured liquid (dye diffusion, colour change, layering)*: light pad or LED panel standing vertically *behind* the row of vessels, with a diffuser (tracing paper, a white shower curtain or baking paper taped to it) filling the frame behind them. This turns the background into an even white field, makes liquid levels and colour fronts read clearly, and hides the room. Keep the liquids pale (dilute dye) so light gets through.
  - *Clear liquids, bubbles, crystals, precipitate*: dark field — black background, two lights behind and to the sides, aimed past the glass, so edges and particles glow.
  - *Plants (seed germination, phototropism)*: one constant light source only, with window light blocked (blackout curtain or a cardboard box around the scene), following Bromhall's principle of total control; a cheap LED grow light or desk LED left on at a fixed setting for the whole run.
- **Kill the glare seen in previous runs**: (1) move the light so it is behind/below the vessel rather than in front; (2) put a black card on either side of the camera and above it so the glass has nothing bright to reflect; (3) turn off room lights and screens facing the glass; (4) tilt the camera a few degrees off square to the glass front; (5) add a polariser only if glare remains.
- The Nuroum V11 has no filter thread (assumption — verify); a polariser would have to be a cheap linear-polariser sheet or a clip-on/handheld filter taped in front of the lens. The XA50 can take a screw-in filter (thread size not verified here).
- **Measurement validity — lighting:** lighting type, position, intensity and polariser angle must stay fixed for the whole run *and* between the control and treatment vessels; any change is a confound (this is the "lamp changes" problem from earlier runs). Backlighting changes absolute intensities but usually *helps* segmentation; it is fine as long as it is constant. A polariser alters measured intensity and colour; if used, record it in the run setup and never rotate it mid-run. Uneven backlight (a pad brighter in the middle) can masquerade as a spatial gradient — check by imaging the empty pad first (a flat-field reference frame).

### Gaps

- No source tested whether specific cheap tracing pads use PWM dimming that flickers at 30 fps; test at the brightness used (record 10 s and look for rolling bands or brightness pulsing).

## Q2. Backgrounds: seamless sweep vs wall; white, black or grey

### Takeaway
A curved seamless sweep (paper or flexible PVC bowed from the wall down onto the desk) removes the wall–desk line so vessels appear to float in an endless field, which is the single fastest way to make desk footage look "studio" rather than "kitchen." Choose white/backlit for bright-field shots of coloured liquids, black for dark-field shots and plants, and neutral grey only when colour accuracy matters more than looks.

### Cited Findings
- The "infinity curve" hides the point where the floor meets the wall, so the viewer cannot find where one ends and the other begins; the result is a smooth transition — [Expert Photography: DIY infinity curve](https://www.expertphotography.com/diy-infinity-curve)
- Shooting on a seamless background avoids time spent retouching out the seam — [Pixelz: DIY product backdrop ideas](https://pixelz.com/blog/diy-easy-product-image-backdrop-ideas)
- For tabletop work, a 4 ft (1.22 m) wide sweep fits most tables; one DIY method is to tape paper high on the wall/ceiling and sweep it down over the table — [Expert Photography](https://www.expertphotography.com/diy-infinity-curve); [Arqspin: product photography background buys and DIYs](https://arqspin.com/product-photography-background-buys-and-diys/)
- Ready-made flexible tabletop sweeps (e.g. MyStudio MS20CYC / MS32CYC) bow to eliminate the horizon line; a retailer Q&A warns that joining two units side by side may still show a seam — [B&H: MyStudio MS20CYC](https://www.bhphotovideo.com/c/product/600445-REG/MyStudio_MS20CYC_MS20CYC_Seamless_Tabletop_Background.html); [B&H: MyStudio MS32CYC](https://www.bhphotovideo.com/c/product/600446-REG/MyStudio_MS32CYC_MS32CYC_Seamless_Tabletop_Background.html); [B&H: MS20CYC 2-pack](https://www.bhphotovideo.com/c/product/600449-REG/MyStudio_MS20CYC_2_MS20CYC_2_Seamless_Tabletop_Background.html)
- Portable, inexpensive seamless background systems are sold for this purpose — [Fstoppers: portable seamless background system](https://fstoppers.com/product/portable-and-inexpensive-seamless-background-system-2974)
- Bright field uses a white or translucent backlit background; dark field uses black — [Sekonic](https://sekonic.com/classroom/whiteline-and-blackline-lighting/); [DIY Photography: dark field](https://www.diyphotography.net/?p=819)
- Bromhall's studio is blackened so only the subject is lit — [PetaPixel](https://petapixel.com/2021/09/04/a-timelapse-of-a-sunflower-opening-over-10-days/)

### Inferences
- **Why a sweep beats the grey wall from earlier runs:** a wall has a visible corner, texture, uneven light falloff and whatever hangs on it; the sweep gives one continuous tone, which looks deliberate and also gives segmentation a clean, uniform background.
- **Cheapest version (inferred):** one sheet of A2/A1 white or black card, or a roll of paper, taped to a box or books behind the vessels and curved down under them (no fold). Matte surfaces only — glossy card reflects the lights. A black velvet/flocked cloth is the darkest dark-field background.
- **Choosing the colour for Argus shots:**
  - White (lit from behind or below): coloured-liquid diffusion, layering, colour change, petri dishes on a pad. Looks clean, "lab-like," and gives the overlays a light canvas — make overlay colours dark/saturated so they show.
  - Black: plants, bubbles, crystals, foams, anything with bright edges; overlays in bright colours (cyan, yellow) pop strongly against black and look "high-tech." Black also hides dust and scratches better on the background.
  - Neutral grey (18% grey card): best if a run measures colour/hue, because the background can double as a white-balance check; visually it reads flatter.
- **Measurement validity:** background colour changes contrast and therefore segmentation; keep the same background for the whole run and for every vessel compared. A backlit white field makes "liquid level" and "dye front" segmentation easier and more repeatable.

### Gaps
- No verified prices for MyStudio sweeps, paper rolls or card in this round.
- No source specifically on how Boxlapse or other scientific timelapse channels build their backgrounds; only Bromhall's general approach was found.

## Q3. Camera settings, interval choice, flicker, stability, rig and infrared

### Takeaway
Turn every "auto" off after the scene is lit and the lights have warmed up: manual exposure, fixed white balance (Kelvin), fixed focus, and the webcam's power-line-frequency (anti-flicker) set to the local mains (60 Hz in North America, 50 Hz in most other places); keep the shutter at a multiple of the mains half-cycle. Pick the interval from the formula interval = event duration ÷ (clip length × fps), and lock the camera to something heavier than a phone stand so it cannot move.

### Cited Findings
**Locking auto modes on a webcam (Windows/UVC)**
- Windows exposes a power-line-frequency property with four states: disabled, 50 Hz, 60 Hz and automatic; it is used when the device does anti-flicker processing for fluorescent environments, and "automatic" is not available on all cameras — [Microsoft: KSPROPERTY_VIDEOPROCAMP_POWERLINE_FREQUENCY](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows-hardware/drivers/stream/ksproperty-videoprocamp-powerline-frequency)
- A suggested order: disable auto, set exposure, tune white balance, then fine-tune brightness; locking exposure manually removes the flickering/pumping that auto mode introduces; on Windows, untick "Auto" next to White Balance and enter a Kelvin value — [Ceedo: adjust webcam exposure](https://www.ceedo.com/how-to-adjust-webcam-exposure/); [Ceedo: exposure and white balance](https://www.ceedo.com/how-to-adjust-webcam-exposure-white-balance/); [Ceedo: webcam settings on Windows](https://www.ceedo.com/how-to-adjust-webcam-settings-windows/)
- OBS users recommend unticking the Focus auto checkbox and turning autofocus off unless you absolutely need it — [OBS forum: getting the most out of your webcam](https://obsproject.com/forum/threads/getting-the-most-out-of-your-webcam.1036/)
- Webcam exposure settings can fail to stick or reset in some drivers/apps — [OBS forum: camera control exposure not working or resetting](https://obsproject.com/forum/threads/webcam-camera-control-exposure-not-working-or-resetting.131179/latest)
- Flicker in camera images typically follows mains frequency; use 1/50 s or 1/100 s in 50 Hz regions and 1/60, 1/120 (or 1/30) s in North America — [Ceedo: adjust webcam exposure](https://www.ceedo.com/how-to-adjust-webcam-exposure/); [Noldus: image from my camera is flickering](https://noldus.com/shared/resources/book/mynoldus-knowledge-base/chapter/related-to-ethovision-xt/page/image-from-my-camera-is-flickering)

**Shutter and flicker**
- Flicker-safe shutter speeds: 60 Hz regions 1/60, 1/120, 1/240, 1/480 s; 50 Hz regions 1/50, 1/100, 1/200, 1/500 s. A 1/48 s shutter (24 fps, 180°) conflicts with 60 Hz mains — [Ulanzi: ambient flicker guide](https://www.ulanzi.com/en-eu/blogs/knowledges/ambient-flicker-high-speed-outdoor-video-guide); [Beverly Boy: avoiding LED banding](https://beverlyboy.com/filmmaking/how-to-avoid-led-banding-issues/); [Swing Catalyst: reduce LED flicker](https://support.swingcatalyst.com/hc/en-us/articles/14758137627548)
- If the scene is too bright at a flicker-safe shutter, reduce light with ND or aperture rather than shortening the shutter — [Ulanzi: ambient flicker guide](https://www.ulanzi.com/en-eu/blogs/knowledges/ambient-flicker-high-speed-outdoor-video-guide)

**Interval selection (the arithmetic)**
- Frames needed = clip length × frame rate (10 s at 24 fps = 240 frames); interval = event duration ÷ frames; shooting time = frames × interval — [Imagen AI: timelapse interval calculator](https://imagen-ai.com/tools/timelapse-interval-calculator/); [Enlaps: how to calculate time-lapse interval](https://enlaps.io/us/guide/time-lapse-calculator.html); [Imagen AI: timelapse duration calculator](https://imagen-ai.com/tools/timelapse-duration-calc/)
- Worked check: 30 s at 30 fps = 900 frames; an 8 s interval over those frames covers about 2 hours — [Expert Photography: time lapse calculator](https://expertphotography.com/time-lapse-calculator)
- The interval must exceed exposure time plus the camera's write time; shooting far more often than needed (e.g. 2 s when 10 s would do) wastes 5× the storage and battery — [Imagen AI: interval calculator](https://imagen-ai.com/tools/timelapse-interval-calculator/)
- No single authoritative rule of thumb beyond this arithmetic was found; vendor calculators are promotional but the formula is standard — (search across the above sources)

**Infrared night recording on the Canon XA50**
- The XA50/XA55 has an infrared mode; the IR lamp is built into the detachable handle unit (HDU-3, included in the box), not the camera body — [Canon XA50 datasheet (German PDF)](https://bpm-media.imgbolt.de/media/pdf/3a/62/c8/DE_Canon_XA50_XA55_Datenblatt5ce3ba103b13a.pdf); [Canon UK store: XA50](https://www.canon.co.uk/store/canon-xa50-camcorder/3669C007/)
- The lamp emits at 850 nm (near-infrared), and IR footage can be monitored/recorded in green or white modes — [Canon Europe: XA55/XA50 specifications](https://www.canon-europe.com/video-cameras/xa55-xa50/specifications/); [Clifton Cameras: XA50](https://www.cliftoncameras.co.uk/canon-xa50-uhd-4k-pro-camcorder)
- Separate from IR, Low Light mode is rated about 0.1 lx at a 1/2 s shutter; the IR light can be assigned to a button — [Canon Europe specifications](https://www.canon-europe.com/video-cameras/xa55-xa50/specifications/); [XA50/XA55 instruction manual (PDF)](https://www.lensrentals.com/product-assets/bedad9ee-0255-414b-8b0a-c6b359b50680/xa50-55-im-n-en.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Webcam lock-down routine (Nuroum V11, inferred):** (1) set up lights, background and vessels; (2) let LED lights run ~10–15 min (warm-up drift is common but not sourced here); (3) in the camera's driver dialog (OBS "Configure Video" / Windows camera properties / Argus's own UVC controls) set power-line frequency to the local mains; (4) untick auto exposure, set exposure so the brightest liquid/background is just below clipping (check the histogram); (5) untick auto white balance and type a Kelvin value matching the lights (e.g. ~5000–5600 K for daylight LEDs); (6) untick autofocus, focus on the vessel fronts, then leave it; (7) disable any "low-light compensation," "backlight compensation" or HDR; (8) record 30 s and scrub it for pumping, banding and focus hunting; (9) save the values into the run record so they can be restored if the driver resets them.
- At 30 fps the frame period is 33.3 ms; at 60 Hz mains the light pulses at 120 Hz (8.33 ms), so exposures of 1/120, 1/60 or 1/30 s each contain whole pulses. At 50 Hz mains use 1/100 or 1/50 s. If the webcam exposes in log2/"exposure value" steps rather than seconds, test each step for banding.
- **Resolution:** record the webcam at its native 1440p MJPEG for analysis; the demo is delivered at 1080p, so 1440p also leaves ~25% room to crop or slowly push in without upscaling. The XA50 in 4K offers even more crop room; 1080p is enough if not cropping.
- **Interval table for likely Argus experiments (computed from the cited formula, 30 fps playback):**
  - 10-min event (food dye spreading in water) → 5 s clip = 150 frames → 4 s interval (150× speed).
  - 30-min event (effervescent tablet, ice melt, density layers settling) → 6 s clip = 180 frames → 10 s interval (300×).
  - 2-h event (crystal growth, slow diffusion in gel) → 8 s clip = 240 frames → 30 s interval (900×).
  - 24-h event (seed germination, mould, yeast rising) → 10 s clip = 300 frames → ~5 min interval (8,640×).
  - Because Argus records continuous video, these can be made afterwards by sampling frames from the stored video (no need to decide in advance), but saving full 30 fps 1440p for 24 h is large; a longer capture interval for long runs saves disk. Shorter intervals give smoother motion; for liquids that move fast (pouring, a tablet fizzing) keep a short real-time or slow-ramp section.
- **Stability / rig:** a phone stand is the weakest link — bumps on the desk shift the frame between frames, which ruins both the timelapse and pixel-based measurements. Use a tripod or clamp on a *separate* surface from where the researcher works if possible; tape or weigh down the tripod feet; route the USB cable with slack and tape it so pulling it cannot tug the camera; don't touch the camera after locking settings (change settings from software); put the vessels on a tray taped to the desk so they can't drift. For top-down petri dishes, a cheap overhead arm or a tripod with a sideways arm, or a cardboard/book "bridge" with a hole for the lens, are low-cost options (not product-verified).
- **Measurement validity — camera:** auto exposure, auto white balance and autofocus all change pixel values or scale over time and look like real changes; they must be off for analysed runs (this is the "auto exposure settling" problem). The XA50's IR mode records monochrome green/white images lit by the IR lamp; colour-based measurements are meaningless in IR, and switching between normal and IR mid-run breaks comparability — use IR only for shape/level/size measurements across the whole run, or for "night" B-roll. The IR lamp is in the detachable handle, so the handle must be attached.

### Gaps
- Nuroum V11 exact UVC control ranges (exposure steps, whether power-line frequency and manual Kelvin WB are exposed) were not verified; check in Windows camera properties or with `ffmpeg -f dshow -list_options true -i video="..."`.
- XA50 IR lamp range/output was not found; Canon's full manual would have it.
- LED warm-up drift duration for cheap panels and tracing pads was not sourced.
- Whether the XA50 can do in-camera interval recording was not checked in this round.

## Q4. Framing and composition for side-by-side vessels

### Takeaway
No dedicated source was found for scientific-vessel composition; the recommendations below are inferences from general product-photography practice plus Argus's own needs (segmentation, calibration, comparison). Symmetry, identical vessels, equal spacing, a calm empty margin for overlays, and a neat matte calibration card in the same plane as the vessels make the shot look designed rather than improvised.

### Cited Findings
- Matte black cards around the subject limit what the glass can reflect and leave only a small gap for the camera, which also tidies the frame — [Sekonic](https://sekonic.com/classroom/whiteline-and-blackline-lighting/)
- A seamless sweep removes the horizon line behind the subject — [Expert Photography: DIY infinity curve](https://www.expertphotography.com/diy-infinity-curve)
- Hackathon guidance for phone/camera footage: shoot landscape, avoid glare and shadows, keep the camera steady on a stand, close enough to see detail — [AI Tinkerers: video production guide for hackathons](https://aitinkerers.org/docs/public/video_production_guide.md?markdown=1)

### Inferences
- **Side view (glasses/jars):** camera lens at the height of the liquid's middle (not looking down), so liquid surfaces appear as flat lines and the vessels don't keystone; use identical vessels in a straight row parallel to the sensor; equal gaps (at least half a vessel width) so segmentation outlines don't touch; odd numbers (3) or a clean pair (control vs treatment) read best; centre the row horizontally.
- **Top view (petri dishes):** camera exactly perpendicular (check with a phone spirit level on the lens front or the dish lid); dishes in a grid with equal spacing; lids off or anti-fog to avoid condensation glare (assumption).
- **Labels:** small, printed, matte labels in a consistent font *below* the liquid line or on the sweep in front of each vessel (e.g. "A — control", "B — salt 5 g"), not handwritten tape; or skip physical labels and let Argus's overlay name the vessels, which looks cleaner and keeps labels in sync with the data.
- **Calibration card:** print it on matte paper (lamination glares), place it flat in the same plane as the measured surfaces (side view: standing at the vessels' front face; top view: at dish height), square to the camera, in a corner where it doesn't overlap any vessel's segmentation region; keep it in frame for the whole run so scale is checkable at any time.
- **Leave margin:** keep ~10–15% empty background at the top or one side for overlay panels/readouts, so overlays never sit on top of the evidence.
- **The arm passing in front:** design interventions so the hand enters from behind the vessels or from above out of frame; or do interventions in a declared intervention window that Argus marks and excludes from measurement; for the demo, cut those frames out of the presentation copy (but keep them in the record).
- **Two cameras:** side view on the XA50 and top/side on the V11 at once gives a natural split-screen and lets the demo show "two views, one measurement."

### Gaps
- No sourced guide on composition for scientific vessel comparisons or how Boxlapse and similar channels frame shots was found in this round.

## Q5. Post-production: deflicker, stabilisation, speed, overlays, split screens, captions, music, grading

### Takeaway
Use ffmpeg (or Resolve) on a *copy* of the footage for presentation only: deflicker (`deflicker` filter), stabilise if needed (two-pass `vidstabdetect`/`vidstabtransform`), build timelapses by frame sampling with a visible "×N speed" label, stack views with `hstack`/`xstack`, and burn in overlays rendered from Argus's measurement records. Music: Pixabay tracks (no attribution required per Pixabay) or YouTube Audio Library tracks (check each track's license type; CC BY tracks need credit). Never feed deflickered, stabilised or graded frames back into analysis.

### Cited Findings
**Deflicker**
- ffmpeg's `deflicker` filter removes temporal frame-to-frame luminance variations; options: `size`/`s` = moving-average window in frames (default 5, range 2–129) and `mode`/`m` = averaging mode (arithmetic `am`, geometric `gm`, harmonic, quadratic, cubic, power, median) — [ffmpeg doxygen: vf_deflicker.c](https://ffmpeg.org/doxygen/8.0/vf__deflicker_8c.html); [ffmpeg-devel: add deflicker filter patch](https://ffmpeg.org/pipermail/ffmpeg-devel/2017-April/210363.html)
- No source in this round said which mode/size works best for timelapse; it needs experiment — (same sources)

**Stabilisation**
- ffmpeg's vid.stab stabilisation runs in two passes: `vidstabdetect` analyses and writes a transforms file (default `transforms.trf`), then `vidstabtransform` applies it, e.g. `ffmpeg -i in.mp4 -vf vidstabdetect=shakiness=10:accuracy=15:result=t.trf -f null -` then `ffmpeg -i in.mp4 -vf vidstabtransform=input=t.trf:smoothing=30:zoom=5 out.mp4` — [Rainnic: stabilize video with ffmpeg and vid.stab](https://rainnic.altervista.org/en/how-stabilize-video-using-ffmpeg-and-vidstab); [ffmpeg-cvslog: vid.stab plugins added](https://ffmpeg.org/pipermail/ffmpeg-cvslog/2013-April/063810.html); [vid.stab on GitHub](https://github.com/georgmartius/vid.stab)
- For soft stabilisation reduce shakiness to 3–4; guides often add `unsharp=5:5:0.8:3:3:0.4` after the transform to counter softening; any filter that changes frame count, order or rate must run identically before both passes — [Rainnic](https://rainnic.altervista.org/en/how-stabilize-video-using-ffmpeg-and-vidstab); [vid.stab GitHub](https://github.com/georgmartius/vid.stab)

**Compositing and background replacement for long plant runs**
- High-end productions sometimes shoot the background separately and composite it with the long plant sequence — [Unique Photo Q&A](https://www.uniquephoto.com/community/qa/how-are-high-end-plant-growth-time-lapses-made-without-wind-and-lighting-changes)

**Music licensing**
- YouTube Audio Library tracks carry one of two license types (shown in YouTube Studio's License type column): the standard Audio Library license (no credit needed) or Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 (credit required, usually pasted into the description from the CC icon) — [vidIQ: YouTube Audio Library rules](https://vidiq.com/ru/blog/post/how-to-best-free-music-youtube-royalty-free); [Creator Essentials: YouTube Audio Library](https://www.creatoressentials.com/glossary/youtube-audio-library/); [Foxi Music: 2026 guide](https://www.foximusic.com/blog/?p=546)
- Sources conflict on using standard-license Audio Library tracks outside YouTube: one says the standard license is limited to YouTube videos (CC BY tracks can go anywhere with credit); a licensing company says to contact the artist for use outside YouTube; another says YouTube's help pages focus on in-YouTube use without a blanket policy for outside use — [LicenseOrg: YouTube Audio Library](https://licenseorg.com/guide/music-audio/youtube-audio-library); [Lickd: YouTube Audio Library](https://lickd.co/youtube-audio-library/); [SocialPilot: YouTube Audio Library](https://www.socialpilot.co/youtube-marketing/youtube-audio-library)
- Pixabay states all its content (including music) is released under the Pixabay License and is safe to use without attribution, even commercially; attribution is optional — [Pixabay FAQ](https://pixabay.com/service/faq/); [Pixabay license text (mirror)](https://open.windriver.com/info/uni-license-list/licenses/pixabay-content.html)
- One third-party blog says some tracks on Pixabay carry Creative Commons terms requiring attribution, so check each track's page — [MusicGPT blog](https://musicgpt.com/blog/truly-safe-copyright-free-music/)

**Speeding up**
- Some hackathon rules forbid speeding up the video to fit the time limit (this refers to the presentation/narration, not necessarily timelapse inserts) — surfaced in hackathon guide search results; specific event not verified — [Tips.hackathon.com: best demo video](https://tips.hackathon.com/article/creating-the-best-demo-video-for-a-hackathon-what-to-know)

### Inferences
- **Presentation pipeline (inferred, keeps evidence untouched):** original video/frames (read-only, analysed) → export presentation copy → optional crop/stabilise → frame-sample to timelapse → deflicker → light grade → burn in overlays rendered *from Argus's stored measurements* → edit. This fits Argus rule 5 (never overwrite evidence).
- **Timelapse by sampling in ffmpeg (inferred example, not from a fetched doc):** keep one frame every N seconds with `-vf "select='not(mod(n\,300))',setpts=N/30/TB"` (every 300th frame of a 30 fps video = one per 10 s) and output at 30 fps; or `-vf "setpts=PTS/300"` with `-r 30` to drop frames. Then `deflicker=size=10:mode=pm` or `mode=median` as a starting point.
- **Speed ramps:** a common pattern is real-time for the setup moment (drop of dye, tablet in water), ramp into high speed for the slow change, and slow back to real-time for the result. Always show a speed badge ("×300", or a running real-time clock from Argus timestamps) so the timelapse is honest; that clock also proves to judges that measurements are tied to real time.
- **Overlays must be consistent:** one font, one stroke width, one colour per vessel used everywhere (video overlays, charts in the app, captions). Render overlays from Argus's own outline/measurement data rather than drawing them in the editor, so what judges see is what Argus measured.
- **Split screens:** `hstack` for two equal views (control | treatment, or side | top), `xstack` for a 2×2 grid; or the app's live view next to the raw video.
- **Captions:** burn in short captions for key claims and numbers (many judges watch muted); sizing ~5% of frame height, bottom-safe area, high contrast.
- **Grading without faking data:** global, uniform adjustments (exposure, contrast, white balance, saturation) on the presentation copy are cosmetic; never use local masks, colour boosts on the vessel content, or "enhancement" that exaggerates the effect being measured, and never feed graded frames to analysis. If the video shows a colour change claim, keep the grade identical across all vessels and the whole clip. A one-line on-screen note ("display copy colour-corrected; analysis on raw frames") pre-empts judge doubts.
- **Deflicker and stabilisation alter pixels** (deflicker rescales frame brightness; stabilisation shifts/zooms/resamples), so they are presentation-only. If the camera really moved during a run, that is a measurement problem for Argus to detect and flag, not something to hide with stabilisation.
- **Music:** for a video submitted outside YouTube (e.g. a Devpost-linked file), Pixabay is the lower-risk free source per its stated license; a YouTube-hosted submission can use Audio Library tracks with the right credit. Keep music low under narration (ducked) and avoid lyrics.
- **Editing tools:** DaVinci Resolve (free version) and CapCut (free) are the usual budget editors; ffmpeg for scripted, reproducible steps. These were not verified in this research round.

## Q6. Screen recording the app (OBS settings, resolution, cursor, pacing for a 3-minute judged demo)

### Takeaway
Record with OBS at a 1920×1080 canvas/output, 30 or 60 fps, CQP ~18, to MKV (then remux to MP4) or OBS 31+ "Hybrid MP4"; enlarge the UI (browser zoom) so text is readable when compressed; and structure the 3 minutes as ~30 s problem/overview, ~1.5–2 min working demo of 3–5 features, short tech/next-step close. Judges may stop at the time limit, and several guides say production polish does not itself score — clarity of the working product does.

### Cited Findings
**OBS settings**
- Record to MKV because a crash leaves a recoverable file, whereas an interrupted MP4 can be lost; convert with File → Remux Recordings or enable "Automatically remux to mp4" in Settings → Advanced; remuxing changes only the container, not quality — [Castr: OBS screen recording](https://castr.com/blog/obs-screen-recording/); [VideoProc: make OBS record in MP4](https://www.videoproc.com/resource/how-to-make-obs-record-in-mp4.htm)
- OBS 31 and later offer a Hybrid MP4 format that removes the remux step — [OBS-versions: recording settings guide](https://obs-versions.com/recording)
- Match the canvas to the monitor's native size; on a 1440p/4K screen keep the canvas native and scale the output to 1920×1080; 60 fps suits tutorials — [Castr](https://castr.com/blog/obs-screen-recording/); [VdoCipher: OBS settings](https://www.vdocipher.com/blog/?p=21072)
- CQP 18 for tutorial recordings and 16 for near-lossless; another guide gives CQP 18–20 for high-quality recording — [OBS-versions: recording guide](https://obs-versions.com/blog/obs-recording-guide); [OBS-versions: recording settings](https://obs-versions.com/recording)
- In OBS, add a Display Capture source and an Audio Input Capture source; close unnecessary applications; on QuickTime enable "Show Mouse Clicks"; test mic levels before recording — [AI Tinkerers: hackathon video production guide](https://aitinkerers.org/docs/public/video_production_guide.md?markdown=1)

**Pacing and structure for a judged demo**
- Keep it 2–4 minutes max; ~30 s project overview; 3–5 key features of the working demo in ~1–2 min; mention tech used; optional next steps; show real functionality rather than slides; production quality does not affect judging there — [AI Tinkerers: video production guide](https://aitinkerers.org/docs/public/video_production_guide.md?markdown=1)
- A suggested arc: problem hook → solution → live demo → impact → ask; avoid opening code editors or terminals because judges care about the product; record a backup — [Reskilll: hackathon demo tips](https://blogs.reskilll.com/hackathon-demo-presentation-tips-pitch-3-minutes-win-2026/); [RevenueCat: pitching your app](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/engineering/how-to-win-shipaton-part-4-pitching-your-app)
- Judges may be instructed to stop watching after the first three minutes, so anything later may never be seen; make sure YouTube/Drive links are public or unlisted — surfaced across hackathon guides in search (exact originating rule not verified) — [Reskilll](https://blogs.reskilll.com/hackathon-demo-presentation-tips-pitch-3-minutes-win-2026/); [DEV: making Devpost submissions not suck](https://dev.to/jacklyn/how-to-make-your-devpost-submissions-not-suck-37i0)
- Title format suggestion "[Hackathon] – [Project] Demo"; unlisted visibility; mention it is a hackathon submission — [AI Tinkerers](https://aitinkerers.org/docs/public/video_production_guide.md?markdown=1)

### Inferences
- **Screen prep (inferred):** set Windows display to 1920×1080 (or record a 1920×1080 browser window) so there is no scaling blur; browser zoom 125–150% so the Next.js dashboard's numbers and notes are readable after YouTube compression; use a clean browser profile (no bookmarks bar, extensions or personal tabs), full-screen (F11) or a borderless window; turn on Windows Focus/Do Not Disturb to stop notifications; hide the taskbar; use realistic, finished data in the app (no "test123").
- **Cursor:** move it slowly and deliberately, park it off the content when not clicking; OBS Display/Window Capture has a "capture cursor" toggle; a click-highlight utility helps judges follow clicks (not verified). Zoom-ins can be added in the editor on key numbers.
- **Avoid waiting on screen:** model calls and long runs should be pre-recorded or cut; show a few seconds of "live" then jump with a visible time-skip label (fits honesty and the 3-minute cap).
- **Suggested 3:00 storyboard for Argus (inferred):** 0:00–0:15 hook — striking timelapse of the experiment with live outlines and numbers; 0:15–0:35 problem (home/school/indie researchers can't measure change cheaply) and one-line answer; 0:35–2:15 the loop on one real run: plan (hypothesis and setup wizard) → watch (live view, overlays) → measure (real units, calibration card, uncertainty) → judge (verdict panel, checks, rivals) → next run suggestion; 2:15–2:40 how it's built (Meta models only interpret; Python computes numbers; claim checker) shown as one diagram; 2:40–3:00 impact and close. Keep a second, contrasting experiment as a 10-second montage if time allows.
- **Audio:** narration recorded separately in a quiet room with a USB mic or the XA50's audio (not verified); music under it at low level; captions burned in.
- **Two recordings to merge:** experiment footage (camcorder/webcam presentation copy) and app screen capture; matching frame rate (30 fps both) avoids judder when cutting between them.

### Gaps
- Official OBS documentation (obsproject.com knowledge base) pages for recording settings were not fetched; the OBS figures come from third-party guides.
- No source in this round on optimal browser zoom, font size or cursor-highlight tools for demo recordings.

## Q7. Common mistakes in timelapse and demo videos (and fixes)

### Takeaway
Most "amateur" looks come from things changing that should not (auto exposure/white balance/focus, daylight, lamps, camera bumps, hands in frame) and from demo videos that are too long, show slides or terminals instead of the product, or have untested audio. Each is cheap to prevent before recording and expensive to fix after.

### Cited Findings
- Auto exposure causes flicker/pumping; lock exposure manually — [Ceedo: adjust webcam exposure](https://www.ceedo.com/how-to-adjust-webcam-exposure/)
- Leaving autofocus on causes hunting; turn it off unless needed — [OBS forum: getting the most out of your webcam](https://obsproject.com/forum/threads/getting-the-most-out-of-your-webcam.1036/)
- Shutter speeds that don't divide into the mains frequency (e.g. 1/48 s at 60 Hz) cause LED flicker/banding — [Beverly Boy: avoiding LED banding](https://beverlyboy.com/filmmaking/how-to-avoid-led-banding-issues/); [Ulanzi](https://www.ulanzi.com/en-eu/blogs/knowledges/ambient-flicker-high-speed-outdoor-video-guide)
- Intervals shorter than exposure + write time fail; intervals far shorter than needed waste storage — [Imagen AI: interval calculator](https://imagen-ai.com/tools/timelapse-interval-calculator/)
- Changing ambient light is the main enemy of plant timelapses; professionals black out the studio and control every exposure — [PetaPixel: Bromhall sunflower](https://petapixel.com/2021/09/04/a-timelapse-of-a-sunflower-opening-over-10-days/); [Unique Photo Q&A](https://www.uniquephoto.com/community/qa/how-are-high-end-plant-growth-time-lapses-made-without-wind-and-lighting-changes)
- Visible seams/horizon lines behind the subject (fixed with a sweep; joined sweeps can still show a line) — [Expert Photography](https://www.expertphotography.com/diy-infinity-curve); [B&H MS20CYC 2-pack](https://www.bhphotovideo.com/c/product/600449-REG/MyStudio_MS20CYC_2_MS20CYC_2_Seamless_Tabletop_Background.html)
- Glare and reflections on glass from uncontrolled surroundings (fixed with flags and polariser) — [Sekonic](https://sekonic.com/classroom/whiteline-and-blackline-lighting/); [NYIP](https://www.nyip.edu/photo-articles/archive/how-to-use-a-polarizing-filter)
- Demo mistakes: too long or too detailed; slides instead of a working demo; distracting apps open; skipped audio test; phone footage with glare, shadows, shake or portrait orientation — [AI Tinkerers](https://aitinkerers.org/docs/public/video_production_guide.md?markdown=1)
- Opening code editors/terminals in a product demo; no backup recording — [Reskilll](https://blogs.reskilll.com/hackathon-demo-presentation-tips-pitch-3-minutes-win-2026/)
- Using a CC BY music track without the required credit turns a free track into a licensing problem — [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/ru/blog/post/how-to-best-free-music-youtube-royalty-free)
- Recording straight to MP4 and losing the file on a crash — [Castr](https://castr.com/blog/obs-screen-recording/)

### Inferences
- **Pre-flight checklist for each filmed run (inferred, 2–3-day plan):**
  - Day 1 (build the set, ~2–3 h): sweep + light pad/LED behind vessels + black flags; blackout window; tripod/clamp; test 30 s clips for flicker at the chosen exposure; print matte labels and calibration card; write down locked camera values.
  - Day 2 (shoot): 2–3 short "hero" runs chosen to finish in 10 min – 2 h (dye diffusion in hot vs cold water, effervescent tablet in different temperatures, salt/sugar crystal or density layers), plus one overnight run (seeds or yeast) on the second camera; no one touches the desk during runs; interventions only in marked windows from behind.
  - Day 3 (edit): presentation copies → timelapse/deflicker → overlays from Argus data → screen recording of the app on the same runs → narration → captions → music → export 1080p, check under 3:00, upload unlisted, watch once muted and once on a phone.
- Don't mix light sources (window + LED + ceiling bulb): different colour temperatures produce colour casts that no single white-balance setting fixes, and daylight drifts over a run.
- Don't let the timelapse imply something it isn't: label speed, keep real timestamps on screen, and keep the original run accessible (e.g. "raw footage and data in repo/release").
- Don't overcrowd overlays: show the numbers that matter for the hypothesis, not every metric at once.

- No verified product prices for any recommended gear; a quick price check on Amazon/B&H is still needed before buying (LED tracing pad, tabletop sweep or paper roll, black foam board for flags, polariser sheet, tripod/clamp, blackout cloth).

# Chemistry, physics, materials and kitchen-science experiments with controls vs treatments for an Argus demo (run-ourselves reference results and open footage)

Scope note: a sibling note already covers open timelapse datasets in depth (`research_notes/Germination videos and cardless measurement/other_experiment_datasets.md`). Its conclusion was that, apart from banana ripening sets, no open measured timelapse datasets exist for dough, crystals, apple browning or ice melt. This note concentrates on reference effect sizes for running the experiments ourselves, and adds only the new footage and data finds. Source quality varies a lot. Peer-reviewed or institutional sources are marked as such. Classroom worksheets, student reports and practice-test items are labelled "low-quality" and should be treated as illustrative only.

## Kitchen/food candidates (dough and sourdough rise, yeast balloon/foam, fruit browning, gummy bears, egg in vinegar, others)

### Takeaway
The best-sourced kitchen candidates are dough/sourdough rise (eLife 2021 measured it by DSLR timelapse: 0.1–1.5 mm/h in 15 mL tubes), apple browning with ascorbic acid vs control (CC BY *Foods* 2020 paper: ΔE measured over 0/30/60 min with a colorimeter, ascorbic acid best and citric acid not significant), and the yeast + sugar balloon (monotonic dose-response with sugar in classroom data). Gummy bear osmosis and egg in vinegar are visual but have only low-quality numeric sources. Banana, avocado, milk curdling, gelatin/agar, chia, bread mould and kombucha were not researched in depth within this budget.

### Cited Findings

#### Dough / sourdough rise in tubes or jars (height, mm)
- **Prior art for camera-measured rise:** Landis et al. 2021 (eLife 10:e61644) recorded dough rise "with a Canon EOS 70D DSLR camera set to image every two minutes for 36 hr". Each dough was 2 g flour, 1600 µL water and 400 µL starter inoculum in 15 mL Falcon tubes (103 mm tall). The dough top was tracked in DLTdv5, height was normalised to tube height and converted to mm/h, and logistic curves were fitted with Growthcurver — [eLife](https://elifesciences.org/articles/61644)
- **Effect sizes:** rise rates ranged **0.1 to 1.5 mm/h** across starters. Starter identity explained rise-rate variation (adjusted R² = 42%, p<0.001) and maximum height (adjusted R² = 22%, p<0.05). Acetic-acid-bacteria abundance correlated negatively with rise rate (ρ = −0.51). The temperature of the rise assay is not stated in the part of the text that was read — [eLife](https://elifesciences.org/articles/61644)
- **Open data:** per-sample rise data are in "Figure 4—source data 3". A Dryad dataset accompanies the paper (doi:10.5061/dryad.0p2ngf1z1); its licence terms were not shown on the fetched page — [eLife](https://elifesciences.org/articles/61644), [Dryad](https://datadryad.org/dataset/doi:10.5061/dryad.0p2ngf1z1)
- **Temperature as the factor:** a University of Copenhagen study tracked dough height at 5, 15, 25 and 35 °C with seven commercial baker's yeasts and fitted first-order kinetics. Rate constants differed between yeast strains. Only the abstract-level description was retrieved; the full paper's server refused connection — [KU Pure listing](https://test01.ku.dk/:obvius/pureproxy/3974268/da/publications/expansion-profiles-of-wheat-doughs-fermented-by-seven-commercial-)
- **Rule of thumb (trade reference):** yeast activity rises with every 10 °C increase in dough temperature up to about 38 °C (100 °F); the sentence giving the size of the increase is cut off in the source — [Correll, Encyclopizza Ch5](https://www.correllconcepts.com/Encyclopizza/Encyclopizza-Ch5.pdf)
- **Protocols:** the Hong Kong exam authority's school-based tasks ask students to measure dough height in cups held at different temperatures (and with different ingredients), logging the temperature every 5 min — [HKEAA temperature task](https://www.hkeaa.edu.hk/DocLibrary/SBA/HKDSE/SampleTask-TL_Food-03-Factors_Affecting_the_Rising_of_Bread_Dough_Temperature-E-Oct17.pdf), [HKEAA ingredients task](https://www.hkeaa.edu.hk/DocLibrary/SBA/HKDSE/SampleTask-TL_Food-02-Factors_Affecting_the_Rising_of_Bread_Dough_Ingredients-E-Oct17.pdf). A protocols.io "Dough Rise Assay" (Oct 2023) exists, but its content could not be retrieved — [protocols.io](https://www.protocols.io/view/dough-rise-assay-c39byr2n.html)
- **Citizen-science context:** the "Science of Sourdough" project (Rob Dunn, launched 2016) explored flour type, time to rise and height of rise — [The Scientist](https://the-scientist.com/bakers-rise-up-to-tackle-sourdough-mysteries-72233)

#### Yeast + sugar balloon or bottle foam (height or balloon size)
- Science Buddies' "Yeasty Beasties" (reprinted by Scientific American) uses dry yeast, sugar and latex balloons over bottles; CO₂ from the yeast inflates the balloons — [Scientific American](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/single-celled-science-yeasty-beasties)
- **Dose-response (low-quality worksheet):** balloon circumference after 15 min was 0 cm with 0 g sugar, 2 cm with 5 g, 6 cm with 10 g and 10 cm with 15 g — [studylib inquiry worksheet](https://studylib.net/doc/25195955/respiration-yeast-metabolism-inquiry)
- A University of Edinburgh "Science at Home" Big Balloon Blow-Up investigation is another protocol — [Edinburgh vet school PDF](https://vet.ed.ac.uk/sites/default/files/2024-09/Science%20at%20Home%20%7C%20Big%20Balloon%20Blow-Up%20Investigation.pdf)

#### Apple-slice browning, lemon / ascorbic acid / salt vs control (colour, ΔE)
- **Best primary source (CC BY):** Shrestha et al., *Foods* 2020, 9(1):76, doi:10.3390/foods9010076, open access under CC BY. Golden Delicious and Elstar slices were exposed to air at room temperature after 1% ascorbic acid (AA), 1% citric acid (CA), or hot-water or steam blanching. For Golden Delicious, ΔE rose from **2.38 (0 min) to 2.68 (30 min) to 4.05 (60 min)**. The control changed more than AA at 30 and 60 min; AA was the best single treatment and **CA had no significant effect on ΔE**. Golden Delicious discoloured more than Elstar. Colour was measured with a Minolta CR-400 (illuminant C, 2° observer, CIE 1976 ΔE), 4 replicates × 2 readings per side — [PMC7022590](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7022590/)
- **Concentration dependence:** a 2009 postharvest abstract tested 0.5–3% ascorbic acid (with and without ultrasound). Inhibition depended on concentration, and 0.5% gave the highest ΔE (most browning) — [PHTNet abstract](https://phtnet.org/research/download/pdf/mg590.pdf)
- **Salt synergy:** a 2015 *LWT* study reported that adding 0.1 mol/L NaCl halved the ascorbate needed for the same shelf life — [Peeref listing](https://peeref.com/works/1018833)
- **Hourly time course exists but numbers not retrieved:** a 2010 Fuji apple study tracked browning at 1, 2, 4, 6, 8, 10 and 12 h and found 1.0% CaCl₂ and 1.0% AA effective — [Scientia Agricultura Sinica](https://www.chinaagrisci.com/CN/Y2010/V43/I16/3402). An older patent recorded L, a, b values on treated apple plugs at 1 to 420 min with a Gardner colorimeter — [US patent 4814192](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/4814192)
- **Days-scale (4 °C) results:** citric + ascorbic acid and CaCl₂ reduced browning and kept higher L* over 5 days; citric acid + CaCl₂ was judged most effective in that study — [Univ. Turin repository](https://iris.unito.it/handle/2318/100497)
- **Lemon-juice mechanism caveat:** a UBC student study notes that citric acid is about 95% of lemon juice's acid content, so ascorbic acid is a minor contributor — [UBC Expedition](https://ojs.library.ubc.ca/index.php/expedition/article/view/197341/192115)
- **Cameras can track this:** a 2011 *Postharvest Biology and Technology* study classified fresh-cut apple browning with a multispectral camera, validated against CIE L*a*b* and a browning index (repository copy is CC BY-NC-ND) — [UPM repository](https://oa.upm.es/6423/). A 2021 *agriTECH* study found a phone camera's R′ channel correlated with colorimeter L* at r = 0.986 (apple juice concentrate, non-enzymatic browning) — [agriTECH](https://journal.ugm.ac.id/agritech/article/view/52956)
- **Consumer claims (secondhand, unverified):** lemon-treated slices stay white for a few hours, and a salt-water soak reportedly kept slices white for more than a day (attributed to Cook's Illustrated) — [PureWow](https://purewow.com/food/how-to-keep-apples-from-browning). A University of Wisconsin CIAS worksheet gives a classroom protocol — [CIAS PDF](https://cias.wisc.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/453/2021/06/Apple-Browning-Experiment.pdf)

#### Gummy bear osmosis (area, colour dilution)
- **Protocols:** soak about 24 h, compare distilled water with salt or sugar solutions (templates use 10% sugar or 10% salt), and compute percentage change in mass or size — [SERC Carleton activity](https://serc.carleton.edu/sp/mnstep/activities/26990.html). A CUNY activity uses distilled water vs 40% salt solution — [CUNY BCC](https://bccbio.commons.gc.cuny.edu/?p=56)
- **Effect size (low-quality):** one middle-school science-fair project reports water-soaked bears grew most, with an average 24.67% change across length, width and height — [Crestwood science fair](https://www.sites.google.com/a/crestmem.edu/science-fair/2015-2016-projects/incredible-growing-gummy-bears). Claims of "doubling or tripling within 24 hours" were found only in unsourced handouts. No peer-reviewed or JCE quantitative source was found.

#### Egg in vinegar ("naked egg")
- **Low-quality student data:** egg mass rose from 79.7 g to 91.7 g (+15%) in vinegar over about 72 h. Corn-syrup results conflict between reports: one shows a small mass loss with circumference growth, another shows 88.2 g to 50.1 g — [Scribd student lab report](https://www.scribd.com/document/554061474/02-04-cell-transport-lab-report), [ABLE poster](https://www.ableweb.org/biologylabs/wp-content/uploads/volumes/vol-34/poster/67_Sluss.pdf), [NY Science Teacher](https://newyorkscienceteacher.com/sci/files/user-submitted/Egg_osmosis.pdf)

#### Banana ripening (colour)
- The Ripen Banana Dataset (Mendeley Data, doi:10.17632/j9sp322drp.1) is CC BY 4.0 and already covered in the sibling note — [Mendeley Data DOI](https://doi.org/10.17632/j9sp322drp.1)

### Inferences
- **Dough rise is the strongest kitchen match for the Argus "height" measurement.** It has peer-reviewed camera-based prior art (eLife) with a published 15× range of rise rates. In 15 mL tubes, 0.1–1.5 mm/h means 0.6–9 mm over 6 h. In wider jars filled deeper, absolute rise in mm should be larger, which is easier to show on video. Temperature (e.g., 20/25/30/35 °C) or sugar (0/2/5/10% of flour) are numeric factors with 3+ levels. Holding several temperatures on a home desk is the practical problem; sugar or yeast dose at room temperature is easier. Cost is under about $10 (flour, yeast, jars); this is an estimate, not sourced.
- **Apple browning fits colour/ΔE but is slow at small ΔE.** In the CC BY paper, ΔE rose only about 1.7 units over 60 min for Golden Delicious, which may sit near a webcam's noise floor. A 2–6 h run, a fast-browning cultivar and an ascorbic acid series (0, 0.25, 0.5, 1, 2%) would give a clearer dose-response. Using lemon juice as "the" treatment is risky, because citric acid alone showed no significant effect in the CC BY paper. Ascorbic acid (vitamin C tablets dissolved in water) is the cleaner treatment. Salt water (0.1 mol/L ≈ 0.6% NaCl) is a cheap second treatment arm.
- **The yeast balloon has a clean monotonic dose-response with sugar** (0/5/10/15 g), and balloon size can be measured as area in a side view. The visual is fun, but balloons sway and can occlude each other; open-bottle foam height (yeast + sugar + a drop of dish soap) is probably easier to segment. That foam version is an inference; no source was found for it.
- **Gummy bears look good** (coloured, translucent, top-down area growth), but no credible effect-size source exists. Run a pilot. Salt or sugar concentration (0/2/5/10%) is a natural numeric factor.
- **Egg in vinegar is too slow (days) for a small change in size**; the best visual is the bubbles in the first hour. Not recommended as the main demo.

### Gaps
- No measured data were retrieved for avocado browning, milk curdling (acid dose vs time), gelatin/agar setting, chia gel, bread mould under conditions, kombucha SCOBY growth, or potato-strip osmosis. Those searches were not run within the tool budget.
- The temperature used in the eLife dough-rise assay, and the Dryad and eLife licences, were not confirmed on the fetched pages. eLife normally publishes under CC BY, but this note did not verify that.
- No hourly room-temperature ΔE or L* table for control vs ascorbic acid apple slices was retrieved. The 2010 Fuji study has such data, but its numbers were not accessible.
- No credible gummy-bear effect sizes were found (Journal of Chemical Education, ChemMatters and RSC were all searched without a hit).

## Chemistry/materials candidates (crystals, chemical gardens, rusting, effervescent tablets, ice melt, celery, hydrogel beads, clock reactions, dye spread)

### Takeaway
Three chemistry candidates combine minutes-scale timing with a numeric factor and sourced effect sizes. The effervescent tablet vs water temperature gives a classroom Arrhenius Ea of about 29 kJ/mol and dissolution times of 130 s at 2.8 °C vs 21 s at 42 °C. Sodium polyacrylate beads swell about 3.4× less by mass in 0.9% saline than in deionised water (patent data). The household vitamin C iodine clock has a supermarket recipe published in *J. Chem. Educ.* (2002) and a modelling paper with data on Dryad (2019). Steel-wool rusting with vinegar or salt is fast and colourful but only has low-quality sources. Crystal growth takes days and has no mm/day reference data. Chemical gardens are spectacular but need corrosive sodium silicate and some toxic metal salts.

### Cited Findings

#### Effervescent tablet dissolution vs water temperature (tablet area, foam/bubble area, time to disappear)
- **Classroom Arrhenius data (University of Waterloo, Chem 13 News):** one Alka-Seltzer in 500 mL distilled water. Times were **130 s at 2.8 °C, 90 s at 13.2 °C, 44 s at 19.3 °C, 31 s at 33.4 °C and 21 s at 42.2 °C**. The fit of ln(1/t) against 1/T was y = −3520.1x + 7.7065 (R² = 0.884), giving **Ea ≈ 29 kJ/mol**. The page notes that above about 40 °C the tablet bubbles on the surface, adding scatter. The procedure text says 1–30 °C, which conflicts with its own table — [Waterloo CASE](https://uwaterloo.ca/centre-advanced-science-education/news/chemistry-alka-seltzer)
- **Second dataset (low-quality, practice-test item):** whole tablets in 200 mL water, unstirred, took about **310 s at 10 °C, 214 s at 25 °C, 142 s at 40 °C and 96 s at 55 °C** — [Test Ninjas ACT practice PDF](https://test-ninjas.com/free/act/test-ninjas-act-science-scientific-investigation-practice-questions.pdf). A sample lab report gives 23 s hot, 40 s room temperature and 102 s ice water — [Scribd sample](https://www.scribd.com/document/355603511/Sample-Lab-Report)
- **Protocols:** Science Buddies Chem_p027 (dissolution time vs water temperature) — [Science Buddies](https://www.sciencebuddies.org/science-fair-projects/project_ideas/Chem_p027.shtml); Chicago ACS (hot, room and ice water, graph time vs temperature) — [Chicago ACS](https://chicagoacs.org/articles/183); Carolina Biological — [Carolina](https://knowledge.carolina.com/discipline/life-science/teaching-the-scientific-method-with-effervescent-tablets/). A patent tested an effervescent tablet at 5, 15 and 20 °C in 200 mL; colder water lengthened dissolution — [US patent 10912847](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/10912847)

#### Sodium polyacrylate hydrogel ("water bead") swelling in salt vs fresh water (area, diameter)
- **Absorption capacity:** sodium polyacrylate absorbs about 800× its weight in distilled water but only about 300× in tap water, and sodium or calcium salts *reverse* the swelling — [Carnegie Mellon Gelfand Center](https://www.cmu.edu/gelfand/k12-educational-resources/polymers/polymer-and-absorption/super-absorb-powder.html). Temple University's demo says over 500× in pure water, less in salt water — [Temple chem demo](https://sites.temple.edu/chemdemo/?p=66)
- **Quantified (patent family, essentially one data source):** at 0 psi, **146.2 g/g in deionised water vs 43.5 g/g in 0.9% NaCl** (104 vs 29.7 g/g at 0.28 psi; 34 vs 24.8 g/g at 0.7 psi) — [US patent 6072101](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/6072101)
- **Peer-reviewed confirmation of dependence:** equilibrium uptake depends strongly on salt concentration and crosslink density (Sohn and Kim, SKKU) — [SKKU Pure](https://pure.skku.edu/en/publications/theoretical-and-experimental-investigation-of-the-swelling-behavi/). A Princeton senior thesis (2021) found swelling in 1 M NaCl much weaker than in pure water — [Princeton DataSpace](https://dataspace.princeton.edu/handle/88435/dsp017d278x12c). An APS DFD 2025 abstract found distilled water gave the largest final swollen volume vs saline, sugar water and other liquids — [APS abstract](https://meetings-archive.aps.org/dfd/2025/e33/16/)
- **Size claim (seller/hobby, unverified):** Orbeez go from 2–3 mm dry to about 14 mm hydrated — [ova.it.com](https://ova.it.com/water-beads/how-much-do-orbeez-expand). RAFT's classroom sheet measures bead diameter in mm over time per liquid — [RAFT PDF](https://raft.net/wp-content/uploads/2019/03/685-Water-Beads.pdf). An ETSU 2024 poster measured diameter and weight at 30 min, 1 h and 2 h in saline, PEG 3350, 2% milk and contrast solution; growth was largest in PEG and smallest in contrast solution, with no numbers in the abstract — [ETSU](https://dc.etsu.edu/asrf/2024/schedule/67)
- **Safety/optics:** the US CPSC counted about 7,000 ER visits for water-bead ingestion (2018–2022) and a 10-month-old's death in 2023 — [Yahoo News/CPSC report](https://news.yahoo.com/water-beads-pose-huge-safety-203806111.html), [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/health/kids-health/health-warning-water-bead-toys-danger-rcna144152). In March 2024 the CPSC warned consumers to stop using Jangostor water beads for acrylamide above federal limits — [CPSC warning](https://www.cpsc.gov/Warnings/2024/CPSC-Warns-Consumers-to-Immediately-Stop-Using-Jangostor-Water-Beads-Due-to-Chemical-Toxicity-Hazard-Violation-of-Federal-Ban-of-Hazardous-Substances-Sold-on-Amazon-com). A Ban Water Beads Act was introduced in November 2023 — [Denver7](https://www.denver7.com/news/national/federal-action-on-water-bead-safety-expected-in-2024-following-safety-concerns-for-children). Major retailers said they would stop selling water beads marketed to children — [AARP](https://www.aarp.org/health/conditions-treatments/info-2023/water-beads-recall-warning.html), [CBS News](https://cbsnews.com/amp/news/product-recall-water-beads-ban-childrens-toys)

#### Household vitamin C iodine clock (colour switch, time-to-switch vs concentration)
- Wright, *J. Chem. Educ.* 2002, 79, 40A uses supermarket items: vitamin C tablets, 2% iodine tincture, 3% hydrogen peroxide and liquid laundry starch. More vitamin C delays the blue-black switch — [JCE 2002 PDF (CSU course copy)](https://diverdi.colostate.edu/C431/experiments/simultaneous%20spectroscopy%20and%20electrochemistry/references/j_chem_ed_2002_v79_p40a.pdf). Vitz's 2007 *JCE* adaptation derives the rate law from timing at varied proportions, with a colorimeter sampling about every 4 s — [JCE 2007 PDF](https://diverdi.colostate.edu/C431/experiments/simultaneous%20spectroscopy%20and%20electrochemistry/references/j_chem_ed_2007_v84_p1156.pdf)
- Kerr, Thomson and Smith 2019 (*Royal Society Open Science*, doi:10.1098/rsos.181367) model switchover time as a function of initial iodine and vitamin C. Their Dryad deposit (19.65 KB zip) contains Excel sheets of experimental results plus Matlab code; the page says content is "licensed for reuse" — [Dryad](https://datadryad.org/stash/dataset/doi:10.5061/dryad.68q4hf7). Further protocols: [Science Friday](https://www.sciencefriday.com/educational-resources/make-a-chemical-clock), [ChemEdX](https://chemedx.org/node/2964), [Flinn worksheet](https://www.flinnsci.com/globalassets/flinn-scientific/all-publication-images-and-pdfs/12610_student1.pdf)

#### Steel wool rusting: salt water vs fresh water vs vinegar (colour ΔE, rust-coloured area)
- **Low-quality student and teacher sources:** vinegar strips the protective coating from steel wool. One write-up reports a vinegar-soaked pad warming from about 27 °C to about 48 °C within 2 min, with visible rust within 3 min. Salt is described as accelerating rusting, and a classroom sheet reports heavy rust on a nail in salt water vs moderate in tap water. A sealed-container O₂ sensor run showed O₂ falling steadily over 24 h — [Scribd assignment](https://www.scribd.com/document/293376791/Assignment-2), [Vanderbilt SVS lesson](https://cdn.vanderbilt.edu/t2-studentorg/studentorg-prd/wp-content/uploads/sites/25/2017/11/Oxidation-of-Iron-mini-lesson-2012.doc), [USF lesson plan](https://etc.usf.edu/fcat5math/teachres/lesplans/sciless.pdf), [Chicago ACS](https://chicagoacs.org/articles/177), [AACT](https://teachchemistry.org/classroom-resources/investigating-fast-and-slow-reaction-rates). Which write-up holds which number could not be pinned down from the search output.

#### Ice melting with salt / sugar / sand (area, meltwater)
- In the Science Buddies activity reprinted by Scientific American, salt-dusted cubes melted faster than sugar, sand and control — [Scientific American](https://scientificamerican.com/article/what-makes-ice-melt-fastest), [Science News Explores](https://www.snexplores.org/article/experiment-what-makes-ice-melt-fastest). A UK school found sand slowest — [Rosary school](https://www.rosary.camden.sch.uk/?p=50004). A student project found sugar faster early and salt ahead after about 30 min — [CYSF project](https://platform.cysf.org/project/7a35ea75-0037-4fff-b1dc-57ae74a99f67). One protocol runs it in a refrigerator to make colligative effects easier to see — [ChildSci](https://childsci.org/test/maybe-its-cold-outside). No replicated timing dataset was found.

#### Celery capillary dye rise (dye-front height, colour)
- Guides measure the cm travelled each hour and compare ice-cold vs warm water; the strongest visual comes after leaving the stalks overnight — [York SOTS](https://www.york.ac.uk/res/sots/activities/celery.htm), [WVU Extension](https://extension.wvu.edu/lawn-gardening-pests/lawn-gardening-pests-learning-activities/how-plants-take-up-water), [NJ lesson](https://www.nj.gov/education/21cclc/casp/lsc/unit1/Lesson8.pdf). #### Crystal growth (alum, salt, sugar, copper sulfate)
- Visible crystals usually take a couple of days to a week — [RSC outreach](https://edu.rsc.org/outreach/crystal-chemistry/1587.article). An IOP Spark classroom recipe uses about 40 g potassium alum per 100 mL warm water — [IOP Spark](https://spark.iop.org/growing-crystal-alum-or-copper-sulfate). #### Chemical gardens (sodium silicate + metal salts)
- RSC rates sodium silicate solution as corrosive and highly alkaline (see the CLEAPSS hazard sheets), and requires goggles and gloves. Manganese(II) sulfate is harmful and dangerous for the environment. **Nickel and cobalt compounds are carcinogenic sensitisers** and should not be used by 11–14 year olds; a colourful garden can be made with less toxic chemicals — [RSC practical](https://edu.rsc.org/download?ac=15257), [RSC 11–14 crystal garden](https://edu.rsc.org/download?ac=512962), [UW–Madison demo lab](https://demolab.chem.wisc.edu/main-group-elements-colorful-stalagmites-the-silicate-garden/)

#### Skittles / candy dye spread vs water temperature (coloured area)
- A UK school found the hot-water colours spread much faster than cold; the result is qualitative, with no numbers — [Holly Grove school blog](https://www.holly-grove.lancs.sch.uk/blog/skittles-science-experiment-hot-vs-cold-water/105485)

### Inferences
- **The effervescent tablet is the best "live, minutes" chemistry demo for a dose-response.** At Ea ≈ 29 kJ/mol the rate rises about 1.5× per 10 °C near room temperature (Arrhenius, my calculation). The 2.8 °C to 42 °C span gives a 6× time ratio, far above plausible replicate noise. Water temperature (e.g., 5/15/25/35/45 °C from fridge, tap and kettle-mixed water) is a numeric factor with 4–5 levels. A top-down camera over clear glasses can measure tablet area shrinking per vessel, and time-to-disappear gives a single response per vessel. Below 50 °C this is home-safe. Cost is a few dollars (estimate). The weakness is that the visual is short (20–300 s per glass), so Argus must sample at 1–2 fps.
- **Water beads have the biggest visual effect of any candidate.** A 3.4× volume ratio (DI vs 0.9% saline) means about 1.5× diameter and about 2.2× projected area (geometry, my calculation). Dry-to-swollen area could grow about 30× if the seller's 2–3 to 14 mm claim holds. NaCl concentration (0/0.25/0.5/1/2%) is a natural Ax factor. Beads swell over hours, which fits a 2–6 h run. **But the CPSC ingestion and acrylamide warnings make water beads poor optics for a public demo by an "AI lab partner" with a safety rule.** If used, show them as adult lab material, say "not a toy" on screen, and avoid child-marketed brands; gummy bears are a safer edible substitute with a weaker effect.
- **The vitamin C clock is visually dramatic** (clear to blue-black, a huge ΔE) and has a clean numeric factor (vitamin C dose, 3+ levels). The response is a switch time, not a gradual curve, and runs take seconds to minutes. Argus's colour-per-vessel tracking would find the switch time directly. Hazards are mild (3% peroxide, iodine tincture stains) but warrant gloves. The Kerr et al. Dryad Excel data could supply reference switch times without running anything.
- **Steel-wool rusting** gives orange-brown ΔE in minutes to hours with salt or vinegar factors, but has only low-quality numbers, and steel wool is hard to segment (fibrous). Iron nails or steel-wool pads on white dishes photographed top-down would be easier.
- **Ice melt** fits "area" (top-down cube footprint) within about 1 h, with salt amount as the factor. Good contrast needs a dark background or dyed ice. The effect is clear in classroom reports but unquantified.
- **Celery dye rise** fits "skeleton length / height of the dye front", but the front is faint inside the stalk until hours have passed. Better as a backup than as the headline.
- **Crystals and chemical gardens** are the most beautiful but fail the "under 6 h" test (crystals) or the home-safe test (chemical gardens with cobalt or nickel). Chemical gardens with less toxic salts could be prerecorded B-roll, flagged "needs care", never "run at home".

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed effect sizes were found for steel-wool rusting rates, ice-melt timing, celery dye rise rates, gummy bears, Skittles spread, or crystal growth in mm/day. All are from classroom sources or unmeasured.
- The Kerr et al. 2019 RSOS paper returned 403 on fetch, so its switch-time values were not extracted. The Dryad licence is described only as "licensed for reuse" (Dryad's usual CC0 was not confirmed).
- Copper sulfate and borax toxicity classifications were not verified in this session. Check SDS and CLEAPSS before recommending either for home use.
- Not researched within budget: paper chromatography, density columns, evaporation of liquids, potato osmosis, elephant toothpaste with 3% peroxide + yeast (foam height vs yeast or peroxide dose), and red-cabbage pH indicator.

## Which are reliably fast (under ~6 h), visually rich, high-effect, and give a numeric factor for a dose-response curve?

### Takeaway
The best fits for Argus's verdict rules (controls vs treatments, at least 2 replicates, a smallest effect that matters) and for Ax (a numeric factor with 3+ levels) are, in order: (1) effervescent tablets vs water temperature (minutes, a 6× effect, sourced Ea); (2) yeast + sugar foam or balloon vs sugar grams (15–60 min, monotonic); (3) dough rise vs sugar, yeast or temperature (2–6 h, peer-reviewed camera prior art); (4) apple browning vs ascorbic acid % (2–6 h, CC BY reference, small ΔE early); (5) hydrogel beads vs NaCl % (hours, large area effect, but poor safety optics); (6) the vitamin C clock vs vitamin C dose (minutes, huge ΔE, response is a switch time). Ice melt vs salt amount and rust vs NaCl % are decent backups with weaker sources.

### Cited Findings
- Effervescent: 130 s (2.8 °C) to 21 s (42.2 °C), Ea ≈ 29 kJ/mol — [Waterloo CASE](https://uwaterloo.ca/centre-advanced-science-education/news/chemistry-alka-seltzer); 310 s (10 °C) to 96 s (55 °C), low-quality — [Test Ninjas](https://test-ninjas.com/free/act/test-ninjas-act-science-scientific-investigation-practice-questions.pdf)
- Yeast balloon: 0/2/6/10 cm circumference at 0/5/10/15 g sugar after 15 min, low-quality — [studylib](https://studylib.net/doc/25195955/respiration-yeast-metabolism-inquiry)
- Dough: 0.1–1.5 mm/h, camera every 2 min for 36 h — [eLife](https://elifesciences.org/articles/61644); temperature 5–35 °C changes the expansion kinetics — [KU](https://test01.ku.dk/:obvius/pureproxy/3974268/da/publications/expansion-profiles-of-wheat-doughs-fermented-by-seven-commercial-)
- Apple: ΔE 2.38 to 4.05 over 60 min (Golden Delicious); ascorbic acid better than control; citric acid not significant — [PMC7022590](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7022590/); concentration-dependent over 0.5–3% AA — [PHTNet](https://phtnet.org/research/download/pdf/mg590.pdf)
- Hydrogel: 146 g/g (DI) vs 43.5 g/g (0.9% NaCl) — [US patent 6072101](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/6072101); salts reverse swelling — [CMU](https://www.cmu.edu/gelfand/k12-educational-resources/polymers/polymer-and-absorption/super-absorb-powder.html); ingestion and acrylamide warnings — [CPSC](https://www.cpsc.gov/Warnings/2024/CPSC-Warns-Consumers-to-Immediately-Stop-Using-Jangostor-Water-Beads-Due-to-Chemical-Toxicity-Hazard-Violation-of-Federal-Ban-of-Hazardous-Substances-Sold-on-Amazon-com)
- Clock: supermarket recipe; vitamin C dose sets switch time — [JCE 2002](https://diverdi.colostate.edu/C431/experiments/simultaneous%20spectroscopy%20and%20electrochemistry/references/j_chem_ed_2002_v79_p40a.pdf), [Dryad](https://datadryad.org/stash/dataset/doi:10.5061/dryad.68q4hf7)

### Inferences
Shortlist. Visual, cost and replicate counts are my judgement; effect sizes are from the sources above.

| Rank | Experiment | Argus measure | Duration | Numeric factor (levels) | Reference effect | Visual appeal | Approx. cost | Safety |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Effervescent tablet vs water temp | tablet area to 0; time to disappear; bubble/foam area | 20 s–5 min per glass; about 30 min total | temperature 5/15/25/35/45 °C | 6× time ratio, Ea ≈ 29 kJ/mol | High (fizz, dramatic) | <$10 | Home-safe below 50 °C; no boiling water |
| 2 | Yeast + sugar foam/balloon | foam height (mm) or balloon area | 15–60 min | sugar 0/5/10/15 g (or warm-water temp) | 0 to 10 cm circumference over 0–15 g in 15 min | High (rising foam) | <$10 | Home-safe |
| 3 | Dough or sourdough rise in jars | height (mm) | 2–6 h | sugar 0/2/5/10%, yeast 0.5/1/2%, or temp | 0.1–1.5 mm/h in tubes; 15× range | Medium-high (timelapse classic) | <$10 | Home-safe |
| 4 | Apple browning | colour ΔE vs card | 1–6 h | ascorbic acid 0/0.25/0.5/1/2% (+ NaCl arm) | ΔE about 1.7 rise in 60 min (control larger than AA) | Medium (subtle early, clear by hours) | <$10 | Home-safe |
| 5 | Hydrogel beads vs NaCl | area (mm²) | 2–6 h | NaCl 0/0.25/0.5/1/2% | about 3.4× mass, about 2.2× area (DI vs 0.9%) | Very high | <$10 | **Ingestion risk; poor optics** |

| 8 | Steel wool / nails in NaCl or vinegar | rust-colour ΔE, area | 10 min–6 h | NaCl 0/1/3.5/7% | qualitative; vinegar pad 27→48 °C in 2 min | Medium-high (orange bloom) | <$10 | Splinters; gloves |

- **Best live 3-minute story:** run the effervescent temperature series live (fast, unambiguous), and cut to a pre-run 4–6 h dough-rise or apple-browning timelapse for the "long run, Ax suggests the next dose" segment.
- **Replicates and noise floor:** with 2 replicates per level, the effervescent and yeast experiments have effect-to-noise margins large enough that verdicts should be unambiguous. Apple browning at under 60 min is the most likely to land inside the noise floor, so run it longer.
- **Dose-response shape for Ax:** effervescent (Arrhenius, monotonic), yeast sugar (likely saturating at high sugar, an inference), hydrogel NaCl (decreasing, saturating), and ascorbic acid (decreasing browning, saturating). All give curves where an Ax "next run" suggestion is meaningful.

### Gaps
- Replicate standard deviations were not found for any of these classroom experiments; the noise floor must come from a pilot run.
- Sugar inhibition of yeast at high concentration (osmotic stress) is plausible but was not sourced here.

## Openly licensed datasets or timelapse videos of these experiments (Wikimedia Commons, Zenodo, GitHub, CC BY supplements)

### Takeaway
Open, measured data exist for only a few of these. The eLife sourdough paper has per-sample dough-rise source data and a Dryad deposit, from camera-tracked tubes. The vitamin C clock has Excel results on Dryad. The CC BY *Foods* 2020 apple paper has ΔE tables and figures. The banana ripening set is CC BY 4.0. A Wikimedia Commons search for time-lapse videos of crystals, rust, chemical gardens, dough, water beads, celery or ice melting returned only climate and satellite footage, plus two micro-scale citric-acid freezing clips.

### Cited Findings
- Commons search (`time-lapse crystal OR rust OR "chemical garden" OR dough OR "water beads" OR celery OR "ice melting"`, video files) returned 33 files, mostly NASA/CIRA sea-ice and ice-sheet animations, two "freezing of micrometer-scaled aqueous citric acid drops" supplementary videos, and one "Ice-melting hg.ogg" clip. There were no kitchen or chemistry timelapses — [Commons API query](https://commons.wikimedia.org/w/api.php?action=query&list=search&srsearch=time-lapse%20crystal%20OR%20rust%20OR%20%22chemical%20garden%22%20OR%20dough%20OR%20%22water%20beads%22%20OR%20celery%20OR%20%22ice%20melting%22%20filetype:video&srnamespace=6&srlimit=50&format=json)

### Inferences
- For the demo video, footage of these experiments will almost certainly have to be **self-recorded**, which also matches the project rule "New work only" and avoids licence risk. The open datasets are best used as **reference results**. For example, Argus could check its own dough-rise mm/h against the eLife 0.1–1.5 mm/h range, or its apple ΔE trajectory against Shrestha et al., and cite them on screen with attribution.
- The eLife dough tubes (15 mL Falcon, DSLR every 2 min) are the closest published analogue to an Argus run. Replicating that setup with a webcam and an ArUco card would make a credible "matches published method" claim.

### Gaps
- Zenodo, figshare, GitHub and Kaggle were not searched specifically for effervescent, hydrogel, rust or clock-reaction videos within the budget.
- The licences of the eLife source data and the Dryad deposits were not confirmed on their pages; check before redistributing.
- YouTube Creative Commons-filtered timelapses were not assessed; the sibling note covers online video licensing.

# Method: foam or growth height

**What is measured.** SAM finds the "foam" in each sample. Argus reports its height in millimetres, straight up the container, sized by the Argus Card. A sample with no foam found reads zero; a picture SAM couldn't outline is a gap.

**Typical curve.** A rise that speeds up, slows and reaches a peak, sometimes followed by a slow collapse as the foam drains. Compare how fast it rises, how high it peaks and when, test samples against the control at the same time.

**What counts as a real change.** A height change above the normal wobble that lasts several readings. A sudden drop to nothing is more often the foam spilling over or a hand in the way than a real collapse; check the events.

**Other possible causes and how to check them.**
- Temperature: warmer samples foam faster; keep controls beside the test samples.
- Evaporation and draining: over long runs the liquid level falls and the foam thins whatever was changed.
- Position in the row: a sample seen at an angle reads its height less reliably; note its place.
- Handling: a bumped or stirred sample foams up for reasons that aren't the test; check the handling events.

# Method: foam or growth height

**What is measured.** SAM finds the "foam" in each vessel. Argus reports its height in millimetres along the vessel's vertical axis, calibrated by the Argus Card. A vessel with no foam found reads zero; a failed segmentation is a gap.

**Typical curve.** A rise that speeds up, slows and reaches a peak, sometimes followed by a slow collapse as the foam drains. Compare the rise rate, the peak and the time to reach it, treatment against control at the same time.

**What counts as a real change.** A height change above the noise floor that persists over several readings. A sudden drop to nothing is more often the foam spilling over or a hand in the way than a real collapse; check the events.

**Rival explanations and their checks.**
- Temperature: warmer vessels foam faster; keep controls beside the treated vessels.
- Evaporation and drainage: over long runs the liquid level falls and the foam thins regardless of treatment.
- Position in the row: a vessel seen at an angle reads its height less reliably; note its place.
- Handling: a bumped or stirred vessel foams up for reasons other than the treatment; check the interventions.
